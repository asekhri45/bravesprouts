Abstract

Social media provides a rich source of data in which to explore large-scale language patterns using automated stress detection. However, the effectiveness of adaptation techniques, model inaccuracies and reliability of model confidence should be explored. Stress classification on Dreaddit is assessed using classical feature representations; fixed pretrained transformer embeddings; training only the classifier head; full fine-tuning; and parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA) with MiniLM, MPNet and DistilBERT. Misclassifications are explored via error analysis and LIME. Calibration and uncertainty are explored via confidence-based selective prediction and temperature scaling. Fixed pretrained embeddings outperform Word2Vec baseline models and adaptation also provides an advantage. A matched ten-seed experiment compared full fine-tuning and MPNet LoRA-large at equal effective batch sizes. MPNet LoRA-large achieved a mean ROC-AUC of 0.913 ± 0.003 and mean F1-score of 0.847 ± 0.005 with about 1.07% of total parameters being updated during training. Full fine-tuning produced a mean ROC-AUC of 0.894 ± 0.016 and mean F1-score of 0.822 ± 0.011. In the original selected-model analysis, there were approximately 2.20 times more false positives than false negatives. There is also evidence of repetition of self-referential and affective vocabulary in the misclassified posts. In the original selected LoRA-large model, temperature scaling reduced ECE from 0.0887 to 0.0343; the matched ten-seed mean decreased from 0.1003 to 0.0434. Using confidence-based selective prediction, there is an increase in accuracy on retained samples as fewer samples are considered. Overall, an integration of parameter-efficient adaptation techniques with reliability, explainability and error analysis can yield effective stress classification. An anonymized reproducibility artifact is available at https://github.com/nlp-artifact-review/stress-nlp-artifact-review.

1Research questions

The study examines how different computational models identify stress in Reddit text and the factors that shape their predictions. The manuscript states four research questions:

  1. RQ1How do pretrained and fine-tuned NLP models compare with classical feature-based approaches for stress detection in Reddit text?
  2. RQ2How do classifier-head-only training, full fine-tuning, and parameter-efficient LoRA compare in predictive performance and parameter efficiency across pretrained transformer models?
  3. RQ3Can error analysis and explainability methods reveal systematic patterns underlying model errors and the factors influencing model predictions?
  4. RQ4How well calibrated are the best-performing fully fine-tuned and parameter-efficient transformer models, and how does confidence-based selective prediction affect the tradeoff between predictive performance and coverage?

2Data

All experiments use Dreaddit (Turcan & McKeown, 2019), a multi-domain Reddit dataset for stress analysis. The study uses the released labeled split of 2,838 training segments and 715 test segments, with binary labels for non-stress and stress/stress-related text. Eighteen redundant duplicate training rows and three training texts that also appeared in the official test set were removed, leaving 2,817 examples, which were split by stratification into 2,394 training and 423 validation examples (85% / 15%).

The official test set (346 non-stress and 369 stress/stress-related segments) was left unchanged and used only for evaluation, not for model training, hyperparameter selection, checkpoint selection, or temperature fitting. No new Reddit content was collected.

3Methodology

Two modeling frameworks are compared on the same binary task. In feature-based classification, text is encoded into fixed representations (skip-gram Word2Vec trained on the training set, or frozen embeddings from MiniLM, MPNet, and DistilBERT) and classified with XGBoost and Random Forest. In end-to-end classification, the same three pretrained transformers are adapted directly with three training strategies of increasing flexibility.

Training strategies

Classifier-head-only
The pretrained backbone is frozen; only the classification head is trained.
Full fine-tuning
All backbone and classification-head parameters are trained (MiniLM 22.7M, DistilBERT 67.0M, MPNet 109.5M trainable parameters).
LoRA
Trainable low-rank updates (W′ = W + (α/r)BA) on the attention query and value projections, with the classification head trainable. Three capacities: LoRA-small (r = 4, α = 8), LoRA-medium (r = 8, α = 16), and LoRA-large (r = 16, α = 32), each with dropout 0.10.

Evaluation protocol

  • Metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, and G-mean (geometric mean of sensitivity and specificity), with stress/stress-related text as the positive class.
  • Initial experiments were run with seeds 42, 123, and 2026. A follow-up compared full fine-tuning and LoRA-large over ten paired seeds with matched effective batch size (eight) and validation-based learning-rate selection, using exact paired sign-flip permutation tests with Holm correction.
  • Model configurations, hyperparameters, and checkpoints were selected on validation performance.
  • Reliability analyses: error analysis and LIME on the fully fine-tuned MPNet model; calibration (Brier score, reliability diagrams, ECE with ten bins) and temperature scaling fit on validation logits; confidence-based selective prediction at thresholds 0.50–0.90.

4Experimental results

4.1 Feature-based classification

The best Word2Vec configuration (XGBoost) reached an average F1-score of 0.731 ± 0.018 and ROC-AUC of 0.773 ± 0.013. Every fixed transformer representation improved on this baseline; DistilBERT embeddings with XGBoost were strongest, with an F1-score of 0.787 ± 0.000.

4.2 Classifier-head-only training and full fine-tuning

Full fine-tuning improved over classifier-head-only training on all three backbones. MiniLM classifier-head-only training tended to predict a single class: in the seed-42 run it labeled 714 of 715 test samples as stress. Among these configurations, fully fine-tuned MPNet was strongest, with ROC-AUC 0.893 ± 0.013, F1-score 0.830 ± 0.009, and accuracy 0.812 ± 0.013.

4.3 Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA)

LoRA trained between 0.17% and 1.31% of model parameters. For MPNet, all three LoRA configurations achieved slightly higher average F1-scores than full fine-tuning; for DistilBERT, LoRA and full fine-tuning were very similar; for MiniLM, full fine-tuning achieved a slightly higher average F1-score than its LoRA configurations. There was no consistent monotonic relationship between LoRA rank and performance across backbones.

4.4 Matched ten-seed comparison

Because full fine-tuning and LoRA used different training settings in the original experiments, a follow-up compared them over ten paired seeds (42, 123, 2026, 7, 13, 101, 202, 404, 777, 1729) at the same effective batch size. For MPNet, LoRA-large attained an F1-score of 0.8473 versus 0.8217 for full fine-tuning, and a ROC-AUC of 0.9125 versus 0.8941.

5Error analysis & explainability

Error analysis used the original selected fully fine-tuned MPNet model (three-seed design). On the 715-example test set it produced 328 true positives, 256 true negatives, 90 false positives, and 41 false negatives: roughly 2.20 times more false positives than false negatives. Because the test set is fairly balanced, this imbalance cannot be readily attributed to class imbalance.

  • False positives averaged 88.99 words versus 79.98 for true negatives, with lower mean confidence (0.882 vs. 0.911) and lower mean sentiment (0.038 vs. 0.134).
  • False negatives had a mean confidence of about 0.839, compared with 0.927 for true positives. Differences in sentence count and Flesch–Kincaid score were not particularly significant.
  • First-person and self-referential unigrams (“I’m,” “I’ve”) and the bigram “feel like” were common in false positives. Variants of “can’t” and “cannot” appeared about 3.24 times per 1,000 words in true positives, 0.59 in true negatives, and 1.62 in false positives.
  • In LIME explanations, “I” contributed toward the stress class in 7 of 10 sampled false positives, and “my” in 2 of 10. Words such as “anxiety,” “panic,” “struggling,” and “emotional” contributed toward stress in examples that were ultimately predicted non-stress, indicating that individual words are not always sufficient to determine a prediction.

LIME provides local approximations of model behavior, not an actual representation of how the model makes its decisions; the manuscript treats these explanations as evidence of the influence of multiple signals, not as evidence that the model relies only on lexical cues.

6Calibration & selective prediction

Incorrect predictions generally had lower confidence than correct ones, but both MPNet configurations produced some highly confident errors (fully fine-tuned: 0.920 mean confidence when correct vs. 0.868 when incorrect; LoRA-large: 0.928 vs. 0.814). Temperature scaling improved calibration without changing predicted labels; accuracy and G-mean were unchanged and ROC-AUC was essentially unchanged.

In the matched ten-seed follow-up, temperature scaling reduced MPNet LoRA-large mean ECE from 0.1003 to 0.0434 (Brier 0.1342 to 0.1237) and MPNet full fine-tuning mean ECE from 0.1415 to 0.0691 (Brier 0.1721 to 0.1499). At a 0.90 threshold, LoRA-large retained on average 47.33% of predictions with 94.74% accuracy, compared with 3.52% retained for full fine-tuning; in 8 of the 10 full fine-tuning runs, no predictions reached a calibrated confidence of 0.90.

Confidence therefore carries some information about prediction correctness, but confidence alone is not sufficient to reliably filter out incorrect predictions.

7Key findings

  1. Representation and task adaptation both matter. The best Word2Vec model reached an average F1-score of 0.731 ± 0.018 and the best fixed transformer representation 0.787 ± 0.000; the strongest MPNet adapted configurations reached average F1-scores between approximately 0.830 and 0.839 across three seeds.
  2. Classifier-head-only training was not consistently strong in the configurations evaluated, which does not establish that frozen representations are inadequate: pooling and classifier-training choices differed between pipelines. Gains from full fine-tuning were smaller for MPNet and DistilBERT than for MiniLM.
  3. LoRA offers a favorable performance–parameter trade-off. Adapting only 0.17%–1.31% of parameters, LoRA was comparable to full fine-tuning for MPNet and very similar for DistilBERT, though not higher for MiniLM. In the matched ten-seed MPNet experiment, LoRA-large outperformed full fine-tuning on F1 (0.847 vs. 0.822) and ROC-AUC (0.913 vs. 0.894), both significant after Holm correction. Higher LoRA rank did not consistently yield better performance, so rank should be chosen on validation data.
  4. Aggregate metrics are insufficient to characterize reliability. Errors were skewed toward false positives, affective and self-referential language appears to contribute to confusion between text that expresses stress and text that merely mentions stress-related topics, and some errors occurred with high confidence. Temperature scaling improved calibration, and selective prediction improved accuracy on retained predictions at the cost of coverage.

8Limitations & scope

Stress-related language detection, not clinical diagnosis

The task identifies text containing stress or stress-related content; it does not diagnose mental health. Dreaddit annotations describe text content, not the author’s mental health state. The study involved no clinical providers or patients, and the manuscript states that such a model should not be used alone in mental health diagnosis, treatment, or any other high-stakes scenario without further human review and validation.

  • One publicly available Reddit dataset and one training–validation split were used, so results do not represent performance across time, communities, populations, or datasets.
  • Initial experiments used three random seeds; only the full fine-tuning versus LoRA-large comparison used ten paired seeds. MPNet full fine-tuning selected the upper boundary of its learning-rate grid, so comparisons do not establish superiority over all possible hyperparameter settings.
  • Only three transformer backbones and one parameter-efficient method were explored. Energy use and broader computational cost were not measured.
  • LIME was applied to a small reproducible sample and does not provide a full picture of model behavior.
  • Uncertainty was assessed only through prediction confidence on in-distribution Dreaddit data; uncertainty from distribution shift, label ambiguity, or model parameters was not assessed.
  • No attempt was made to identify, contact, or re-identify any Reddit author.

9Reproducibility

An anonymized reproducibility artifact is available at github.com/nlp-artifact-review/stress-nlp-artifact-review. According to the manuscript, it includes the full experimental notebook, analysis pipeline, pinned Python environment, dataset-access instructions, a CPU-only script that recalculates ten-seed statistics from archived predictions, and a GPU script that reruns the matched full fine-tuning and LoRA-large configurations.

Transformer experiments ran in Google Colab on an NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB GPU with PyTorch 2.11.0 (CUDA 13.0), Transformers 5.16.1, Sentence-Transformers 6.0.1, Datasets 4.8.4, PEFT 0.20.0, Accelerate 1.14.0, and PyArrow 23.0.1. The manuscript’s AI-use statement is given in its Section X.

About this page

This research is by Aarav Sekhri, founder of MyBraveSprout, which builds free, parent-guided conversation activities for children with selective mutism. The study reflects a broader interest in how computation can support communication and mental health. It stands apart from the MyBraveSprout app: it uses only the public Dreaddit dataset, and no MyBraveSprout user data was involved.
