Abstract

Social media provides a rich source of data in which to explore large-scale language patterns using automated stress detection. However, the effectiveness of adaptation techniques, model inaccuracies and reliability of model confidence should be explored. Stress classification on Dreaddit is assessed using classical feature representations; fixed pretrained transformer embeddings; training only the classifier head; full fine-tuning; and parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA) with MiniLM, MPNet and DistilBERT. Misclassifications are explored via error analysis and LIME. Calibration and uncertainty are explored via confidence-based selective prediction and temperature scaling. Fixed pretrained embeddings outperform Word2Vec baseline models and adaptation also provides an advantage. A matched ten-seed experiment compared full fine-tuning and MPNet LoRA-large at equal effective batch sizes. MPNet LoRA-large achieved a mean ROC-AUC of 0.913 ± 0.003 and mean F1-score of 0.847 ± 0.005 with about 1.07% of total parameters being updated during training. Full fine-tuning produced a mean ROC-AUC of 0.894 ± 0.016 and mean F1-score of 0.822 ± 0.011. In the original selected-model analysis, there were approximately 2.20 times more false positives than false negatives. There is also evidence of repetition of self-referential and affective vocabulary in the misclassified posts. In the original selected LoRA-large model, temperature scaling reduced ECE from 0.0887 to 0.0343; the matched ten-seed mean decreased from 0.1003 to 0.0434. Using confidence-based selective prediction, there is an increase in accuracy on retained samples as fewer samples are considered. Overall, an integration of parameter-efficient adaptation techniques with reliability, explainability and error analysis can yield effective stress classification. An anonymized reproducibility artifact is available at https://github.com/nlp-artifact-review/stress-nlp-artifact-review.
